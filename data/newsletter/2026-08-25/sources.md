## Section 1 — The problem + the classical foundations (KTH lineage)

### Cell Tracking Challenge — the objective benchmark — VERIFIED
- Ulman, V. … Kozubek, M. & Ortiz-de-Solórzano, C. "An objective comparison of cell-tracking
  algorithms." *Nature Methods* **14**(12):1141–1152, 2017. DOI: 10.1038/nmeth.4473 · PMID: 29083403.
- Senior authors: Michal Kozubek & Carlos Ortiz-de-Solórzano (final two). **KTH note: Klas E. G.
  Magnusson AND Joakim Jaldén are both among the 39 authors** — KTH helped build the benchmark.
- ABSTRACT-VERIFIED (verbatim): "**21 participating algorithms and a data repository consisting of
  13 data sets from various microscopy modalities**"; "**none obtained fully correct solutions**";
  "methods that either **take prior information into account using learning strategies** or **analyze
  cells in a global spatiotemporal video context** performed better than other methods."
- FLAG: this 2017 paper benchmarks tracking algorithms *generally* (its finding: learning-based OR
  global-context methods led) — do NOT call it a "deep-learning" paper. The DL shift is framed in the
  2023 update below.

### Cell Tracking Challenge — 10-year update (review anchor) — VERIFIED
- Maška, M. … Kozubek, M. & Ortiz-de-Solórzano, C. "The Cell Tracking Challenge: 10 years of
  objective benchmarking." *Nature Methods* **20**(7):1010–1020, 2023.
  DOI: 10.1038/s41592-023-01879-y · PMID: 37202537. (Klas E. G. Magnusson again among authors.)
- ABSTRACT-VERIFIED (verbatim): "an ongoing benchmarking initiative that has become **a reference in
  cell segmentation and tracking algorithm development**"; created "a **silver standard reference
  corpus** based on the most competitive results, which will be of particular interest for
  **data-hungry deep learning-based strategies**"; adds "two novel, insightful studies about the
  **generalizability and the reusability** of top-performing methods."

### Magnusson & Jaldén — Viterbi global-linking tracker (classical; KTH) — VERIFIED
- Magnusson, K.E.G., Jaldén, J., Gilbert, P.M. & Blau, H.M. "Global Linking of Cell Tracks Using the
  Viterbi Algorithm." *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging* **34**(4):911–929, 2015.
  DOI: 10.1109/TMI.2014.2370951 · PMID: 25415983. (Exactly 4 authors — NOT "et al." Magnusson &
  **Jaldén both KTH** at the time.)
- ABSTRACT-VERIFIED (verbatim): "a **global track linking algorithm, which links cell outlines
  generated by a segmentation algorithm into tracks**"; links using "information from the complete
  image sequence in every linking decision," "finding the tracks which give the largest possible
  increases to a probabilistically motivated scoring function, **using the Viterbi algorithm**"; a
  "novel way to alter previously created tracks when new tracks are created, thus **mitigating the
  effects of error propagation**"; "can handle **mitosis, apoptosis, and migration in and out of the
  imaged area**," plus "false positives, missed detections, and clusters of jointly segmented cells."
- This is a CLASSICAL (non-deep-learning) global-optimization tracker — the pre-DL state of the art.

## Section 2 — Deep learning takes over the linking

### TrackMate 7 — DL detectors into an open tracking pipeline — VERIFIED
- Ershov, D. … Jacquemet, G. & Tinevez, J.-Y. "TrackMate 7: integrating state-of-the-art
  segmentation algorithms into tracking pipelines." *Nature Methods* **19**:829–832, 2022.
  DOI: 10.1038/s41592-022-01507-1 · PMID: 35654950. First author Dmitry Ershov; senior author
  Jean-Yves Tinevez.
- ABSTRACT-VERIFIED (verbatim): "TrackMate is an automated tracking software… **distributed as a Fiji
  plugin**"; TrackMate 7 is "built to address the broad spectrum of modern challenges… by
  **integrating state-of-the-art segmentation algorithms into tracking pipelines**"; shown to
  "**function effectively across a wide range of bio-imaging experiments**."
- FLAG (BODY-ONLY): the specific DL detector names (StarDist, Cellpose) are NOT in the abstract — do
  not quote them as abstract. The "open / Fiji plugin" claim IS abstract-verified.

### Caliban / DeepCell — DL tracking + lineage — VERIFIED (PREPRINT — label as such)
- Schwartz, M.S. … Van Valen, D. "Caliban: Accurate cell tracking and lineage construction in
  live-cell imaging experiments with deep learning." **bioRxiv**, 2019 (5 versions through 2024).
  DOI: 10.1101/803205. First author Morgan Schwartz; senior author David Van Valen. **PREPRINT —
  no peer-reviewed version found; state as bioRxiv.** (Do NOT conflate with the distinct peer-reviewed
  "DeepCell Kiosk," Bannon et al., Nat Methods 2021.)
- ABSTRACT-VERIFIED (verbatim): cell tracking performance "has been **limited by a lack of dynamic
  datasets with temporally consistent single-cell labels**"; presents "a new framework for scalable,
  **human-in-the-loop labeling of live-cell imaging movies**" and a DL tracker that "**achieves
  state-of-the-art performance in cell tracking**"; "all of the data, code, and software publicly
  available with permissive open-source licensing through the DeepCell project's web portal."

### Trackastra — transformer-based association — VERIFIED (arXiv; accepted ECCV 2024)
- Gallusser, B. & Weigert, M. "Trackastra: Transformer-based cell tracking for live-cell microscopy."
  **arXiv:2405.15700**, 2024; comment line: "Accepted at ECCV 2024." (No proceedings DOI confirmable;
  cite as arXiv / ECCV 2024.)
- ABSTRACT-VERIFIED (verbatim): existing linking "is commonly solved via **discrete optimization
  methods, which require manual tuning of hyperparameters for each dataset**"; Trackastra "uses a
  **simple transformer architecture to directly learn pairwise associations of cells within a
  temporal window** from annotated data"; "unlike existing transformer-based MOT pipelines, our
  learning architecture also **accounts for dividing objects such as cells**" and "allows for
  **accurate tracking even with simple greedy linking**"; "performs **on par with or better than
  highly tuned state-of-the-art cell tracking algorithms**" across "bacteria, cell cultures and
  fluorescent particles."

## Section 3 — The honest frontier + lab hook
- Frontier: cells DIVIDE (one becomes two) — tracking is not generic object tracking; errors
  PROPAGATE over long movies (Viterbi paper explicitly mitigates "error propagation"); classical
  linking needs per-dataset hyperparameter tuning (Trackastra); the benchmark's own verdict was
  "none obtained fully correct solutions" (2017), and generalizability/reusability remain open
  questions (2023). A track is a HYPOTHESIS about a cell's identity through time until ground truth
  agrees — the prove-it discipline (/post/newsletter-2026-07-27/).
- Open/callable tools: TrackMate (Fiji), DeepCell/Caliban (open, deepcell.org), Trackastra (code) —
  the BioImage Model Zoo (/project/bioimage-model-zoo/) / ImJoy (/project/imjoy/) /
  BioEngine (/project/bioengine/) ethos; the Cell Tracking Challenge IS the objective benchmark.
- Lab hook: tracking is the temporal spine of live-cell experiments — REEF (/project/reef-imaging-farm/),
  the self-driving microscope (/project/self-driving-microscope/), Agent-Lens (/project/agent-lens/).
  KTH lineage: Jaldén & Magnusson helped build the field; Jaldén is our collaborator on the newly
  added smart-microscopy bandits preprint (arXiv:2512.14930), which decides when/where to image
  "dynamically evolving regions" — tracking is what tells you a region is evolving. The
  video-diffusion time-lapse preprint (arXiv:2503.18583) generates cell-division movies — tracking
  is how you measure division in real ones.
- Pipeline connections: segment (/post/newsletter-2026-08-16/) → track (today) → choose the moment
  (/post/newsletter-2026-08-08/); restoration (/post/newsletter-2026-08-22/) gives gentler, longer
  movies = more to track; a track is the raw substrate of cell dynamics a virtual cell
  (/post/newsletter-2026-08-15/) would model.
